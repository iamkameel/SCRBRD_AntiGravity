Pptimise for **system leverage**, not just match-day beauty.  That means a hard shift in thinking:  **SCRBRD is not a scoring screen.** It is a **school cricket operating system** that happens to include scoring.  The UI work must be strong, but if you stop there, you risk building a polished front end on top of a shallow product. The right ambition is to visually, especially around live scoring, wagon wheel interaction, innings flow, and intelligence panels, but it is still fundamentally centred on the **match UI layer** rather than the full **school cricket platform layer**.   ## What “operating system for school cricket” actually means  An operating system is not just a tool. It becomes the **default environment** where cricket operations happen.  For SCRBRD, that means six connected layers:  ### 1. Competition layer  This is where the structure of cricket lives:  * seasons * leagues * cups * friendlies * divisions * age groups * fixtures * venues * officials  Without this, scoring is isolated. With it, every score updates a competition ecosystem.  ### 2. Team operations layer  This is the school and coach workflow:  * squad selection * team sheets * batting order * bowling options * player availability * transport * match readiness * role assignments  This is where schools actually “run cricket”, not just record it.  ### 3. Match engine layer  This is the scoring core:  * innings state * strike logic * extras * wickets * over changes * declarations * DLS or reduced overs later * wagon wheel * partnerships * scorecards  This must be the most reliable part of the whole system.  ### 4. Analytics and intelligence layer  This is where SCRBRD becomes differentiated:  * player form * bowling trends * scoring zones * powerplay patterns * head-to-heads * phase performance * school-wide reports * coach alerts * development insights  This is the layer that turns raw scoring into decision-making.  ### 5. Identity and profile layer  This is where every person becomes a persistent entity:  * player profiles * coach profiles * schools * teams * season stats * awards * milestones * injuries * development notes  Without this, every match starts from zero. With it, every match adds to a player journey.  ### 6. Communication and administration layer  This is the unglamorous but sticky layer:  * notifications * reminders * fixture confirmations * availability prompts * invoices * disciplinary records * media * documents * audit logs  This is what makes the platform hard to leave.  ## The most important product truth  You need to avoid a common trap:  > Mistaking the most visible part of the product for the most valuable part.  The scoring hub is the most visible. The **data network** is the most valuable.  If you get the network right, SCRBRD becomes:  * the source of truth for schools * the match archive * the player development record * the competition engine * the analytics layer * the reporting tool  That is operating-system territory.  ## The strategic moat  Your moat is not that you can draw a wagon wheel better.  Your moat is that you can connect:  **school → squad → fixture → line-up → scoring → analytics → player development → reporting**  Most products do one or two of these well. Very few connect all of them in a school-cricket-first way.  That is the opening.  ## Where your current thinking is strongest  You already think in systems:  * role assignments * match flow * player detail * live intelligence * school dashboards * scoring-to-analytics linkage  That is the right instinct.  ## Where I’d challenge you  You may still be overweighting:  * premium visual design * broadcast energy * live-match theatre  Those matter, but they are not the centre of gravity.  For schools, the real buying and adoption drivers are:  * reliability * speed of setup * ease of scoring * coach usefulness * player history * admin simplicity * competition reporting  A beautiful interface helps adoption. A deeply embedded workflow creates dependence.  ## The right product hierarchy  If the goal is truly “the operating system for school cricket”, your priority stack should be:  ### Tier 1 — Must be world-class  * fixture creation * team selection * line-up confirmation * live scoring * automatic scorecards * player and team stats * competition tables  ### Tier 2 — Creates stickiness  * coach dashboards * player development logs * awards and accolades * availability * notifications * school admin workflows  ### Tier 3 — Creates differentiation  * match intelligence * predictive insights * wagon wheel heatmaps * comparison tools * scouting views * broadcast overlays  That order matters.  ## The best framing for SCRBRD  Not: **“a cricket scoring app”**  Not even: **“a live scoring and analytics platform”**  But: **“the digital infrastructure layer for school cricket”**  That framing is stronger because it suggests:  * permanence * interoperability * institutional value * long-term data compounding  ## The architecture implication  You should structure the product as four engines:  ### 1. Competition Engine  Manages seasons, leagues, teams, fixtures, standings.  ### 2. Match Engine  Handles toss, innings state, ball-by-ball scoring, scorecards.  ### 3. Identity Engine  Manages persons, roles, player histories, schools, teams.  ### 4. Intelligence Engine  Transforms events into analytics, insights, trends, reports.  The UI should sit on top of those engines, not substitute for them.  ## The real end state  When SCRBRD is mature, a school should be able to say:  * “We schedule all our cricket here.” * “We select teams here.” * “We score matches here.” * “We analyse players here.” * “We manage records here.” * “We report performance here.” * “Parents and players follow results here.”  Once that happens, leaving the platform becomes painful.
